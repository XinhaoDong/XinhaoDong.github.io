About

I am a PhD candidate in Economics at Simon Fraser University, supervised by Lucas Herrenbrueck.

My research interests lie in macroeconomic theory, monetary policy, and family economics. I study how policies transmit through banks and households. On the monetary side, I analyze China’s 2015 deposit-rate liberalization and reserve-requirement adjustments to quantify pass-through and develop structural models of deposit pricing and bank competition. On the family side, I develop overlapping-generations (OLG) frameworks and use micro data to examine how fertility, education, and pensions interact with policy and demographics.

I also collaborate with computer science researchers on game-theoretic models of censorship and circumvention. Outside of research, I enjoy photography.

Research

Financial Liberalization and the Effectiveness of Reserve Policy: Evidence from China’s 2015 Deposit Rate Reform

Working paper (2025). After the removal of the deposit-rate ceiling, bank deposit rates become more responsive to RRR changes, while average deposit rates decline. I provide bank-level difference-in-differences evidence and a structural deposit-pricing model with reserve requirements that rationalizes both findings.

Pension Promises, Fertility, and Debt Sustainability

Working paper (2026). I study how pension policy affects fertility and, in turn, the long-run sustainability of public debt in overlapping-generations economies.
