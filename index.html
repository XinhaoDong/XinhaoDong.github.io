About

Research shaped by real institutions.

I am supervised by Lucas Herrenbrueck. My work combines empirical and theoretical approaches to monetary economics, household finance, and social policy, with a focus on the institutions that shape financial and family decisions.

My research examines bank funding and reserve requirements, revolving credit, and the value of liquidity. I also study how public policy shapes social protection and family decisions, especially in China. An interdisciplinary project explores the strategic interaction between censorship and circumvention.

Macroeconomics & Finance

Working paper · 2026

Deposit Pricing, Bank Funding Models, and the Incidence of Reserve Requirements

Earlier version: Financial Liberalization and the Effectiveness of Reserve Policy: Evidence from China’s 2015 Deposit Rate Reform

Read summary

Using quarterly data for Chinese banks from 2008 to 2024, I compare the relationship between banks’ pre-reform deposit mix, reserve requirements, and market funding across pricing regimes. Among joint-stock banks, the fitted funding gradient falls from 1.144 to −0.024 percentage points per percentage-point increase in the reserve ratio. Limited common support and sensitivity to exposure trends prevent a causal interpretation of that regime difference.

Working paper · 2026

Urgency, Liquidity, and the Price of Convenience

With Lucas Herrenbrueck and Zijian Wang

Read summary

We distinguish the urgency of spending needs from the liquidity services of assets using Treasury bills, commercial paper, and AAA corporate bonds. Our headline estimate puts the annual price of convenience at 5.5%, far above conventional spread measures; only about 23% of it is attributable to urgency.

Working paper · 2026

Existing Credit, New Credit, and Monetary Transmission

Read summary

Monetary tightening reprices credit-card borrowing and weakens initial limits on new accounts, but leaves previously authorized lines broadly intact across the limit distribution, line cuts, account exit, and line increases. Households draw more heavily on those lines as utilization and delinquency rise. A short-run accounting exercise bounds the contribution of withdrawing existing credit to the modeled spending response.

Households & Social Protection

Working paper · 2026

Who Gets Protection? Geographic Inequality in Social Protection after Land Expropriation in China

Read summary

Using China Household Income Project surveys, I compare the protection rural households received after land expropriation. Among earlier cohorts, households in fiscally weaker provinces were about 20 percentage points less likely to receive durable protection and more likely to receive cash alone. Those gaps are close to zero among later cohorts, while cross-province dispersion in protection falls by about one-third.

Working paper · 2026

Pensions, Migration, and Three-Generation Family Reorganization

With Yang Li

Read summary

Using age-60 eligibility around China’s 2016 pension reform, we find that pension access raises grandchild care by 8.2 percentage points. Grandchildren then spend fewer months with their parents, while complete paternal absence falls and shorter, more returnable work trips become more common. Remittances from pension recipients to adult children also decline, consistent with a shift in how families provide informal support.

Working paper · 2026

Strategic Obfuscation against Adaptive Censorship: Equilibrium Blocking and Dynamic Implementability

With Yuqi Hu

Read summary

We model costly traffic obfuscation and an adaptive censor’s blocking decision jointly. Exact unilateral best responses determine which obfuscation–blocking targets can survive repeated interaction; a common shortcut that checks only complete abandonment of obfuscation can misclassify whether a target is sustainable. A public-signal extension shows how missed detections and false alarms tighten these conditions.
