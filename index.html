About

Research shaped by real institutions.

I am supervised by Lucas Herrenbrueck. My research lies at the intersection of macroeconomic theory, monetary policy, and family economics, with a particular focus on how policy is transmitted through banks, families, and intergenerational institutions.

One strand of my work studies monetary transmission and financial liberalization. A second examines fertility, education, pensions, migration, and public-debt sustainability in overlapping-generations economies. I also work on interdisciplinary projects in political economy and computer science.

Working paper · 2025

Financial Liberalization and the Effectiveness of Reserve Policy: Evidence from China’s 2015 Deposit Rate Reform

Read abstract

This paper studies how the removal of China’s deposit-rate ceiling changed the transmission of reserve-requirement-ratio policy through the banking system. Using bank-level difference-in-differences evidence, I show that deposit rates became more responsive to RRR changes after liberalization, even as average deposit rates declined. A structural deposit-pricing model highlights stronger monetary transmission through the deposit channel alongside greater pressure on bank profitability.

Working paper · 2026

Pension Promises, Fertility, and Debt Sustainability

Read abstract

This paper studies how pension promises affect fertility decisions and the long-run sustainability of public debt. An overlapping-generations model with endogenous fertility, pay-as-you-go pensions, and debt rollover can feature multiple demographic steady states, linking pension design to the stability of the future tax base.

Working paper · 2026

Interest Rates, Moneyness, and the Fisher Equation

With Lucas Herrenbrueck and Zijian Wang

Read abstract

We revisit the Fisher equation by distinguishing between a hypothetical perfectly illiquid safe asset and the liquid safe assets used in monetary-policy implementation. The framework clarifies how liquidity and collateral services affect the relationship between nominal rates, inflation, consumption growth, and asset prices.

Working paper · 2025

Strategic Obfuscation under Adaptive Censorship: A Game-Theoretic Framework

With Yuqi Hu

Read abstract

This paper develops a game-theoretic framework for censorship and circumvention as an adaptive strategic interaction. It characterizes equilibria in one-shot and repeated settings and shows how traffic variance and seasonality can become strategic tools when risk-averse censors face classification errors and collateral damage.

Pensions, Migration, and Three-Generation Family Reorganization

With Yang Li

Read abstract

Using CHARLS panel data, we study how pension integration affects household organization, migration, and elderly well-being in rural China. The project links pension reform to three-generation family arrangements and the allocation of caregiving responsibilities.

Working paper · 2026

Revealed Son Preference and Fertility Continuation in Low-Fertility China

Read abstract

Using China Family Panel Studies household rosters from 2010 to 2022, I reconstruct mother-level fertility histories and find that son preference remains behaviorally important in low-fertility China. The continuation response is concentrated among rural, agricultural-hukou, lower-education, lower-income, and earlier-cohort families.
